Let us go deeper into the issue.

Many small island communities face serious water challenges due to their limited freshwater resources. On some islands, such as those in the Pacific and Caribbean, rainfall is the main source of drinking water, but changing weather patterns and prolonged droughts caused by climate change have made supplies unreliable. Over-extraction of groundwater often leads to saltwater intrusion, making the water unsafe for consumption. Pollution from poor waste management and agricultural runoff further reduces the quality of available water. With growing populations and tourism increasing demand, these islands must adopt sustainable water management practices—such as rainwater harvesting, desalination, and conservation education—to secure a safe and reliable water supply for the future.

Save Water

Every Drop Counts

At Home

  • Close the tap when not in use
  • Take shorter showers
  • Fix leaks as soon as you see them
  • Run machines when they're full

In the Garden

  • Water early morning or evening when it's cooler
  • Collect rainwater for watering plants
  • Use water carefully for plants
  • Avoid waste during cleaning

At School

  • Report a leaking tap to an adult
  • Don't let water run unnecessarily
  • Raise awareness among classmates about good practices
  • Participate in water-related projects

In the Neighborhood

  • Share good practices with others
  • Protect water points and green spaces
  • Avoid throwing waste in nature
  • Act together to save water
